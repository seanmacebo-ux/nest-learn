Water

Your tap water in Edenvale travels from dams in Lesotho, through the Vaal system, to Rand Water's treatment plants, then through Ekurhuleni's pipes to your flat. It is tested against a national standard and Ekurhuleni scores well. The weak spot is supply, not quality, so the smart moves are a small water store and, if you want better taste, a simple filter.

🧭 The system

The same four questions for every track. Follow the money, then decide.

💸Who pays
  • Ekurhuleni households and businesses pay municipal water charges, while the city buys treated bulk water from Rand Water.
  • The bill supports several layers: raw-water infrastructure, treatment, electricity for pumping, local pipes and reservoirs, and administration.
  • Sanitation is a separate service charge, and ERWAT treats much of Ekurhuleni's wastewater on the city's behalf.
  • National grants and municipal support policies help fund infrastructure and services for qualifying poor households, so access to a basic allocation does not make treatment costless.
  • Outages create a second household bill through bottled water, tanks, pumps, filters, maintenance and time spent collecting water.
⚖️Who decides
  • The Department of Water and Sanitation [DWS] manages national water resources, regulates water use and oversees drinking-water and wastewater performance.
  • South Africa and Lesotho govern their shared Highlands project through a treaty, while LHDA implements the Lesotho-side works and TCTA has South African project responsibilities.
  • Rand Water treats and transports bulk water, while Ekurhuleni is the water services authority [the municipality responsible for ensuring local access].
  • The municipal council approves retail tariffs and budgets after public consultation, and city departments operate local distribution and approve borehole applications.
  • SANS 241 [South Africa's drinking-water standard] defines quality and management requirements, but a published standard needs sampling, competent operators and enforcement to protect a particular tap.
🏦Who profits
  • Rand Water is a public utility, so its revenue and surplus must be distinguished from a private company's shareholder profit.
  • Lesotho receives treaty royalties, and its electricity system benefits from hydropower associated with the project.
  • Construction companies, engineering firms, electricity suppliers, lenders and chemical suppliers earn revenue from building and operating water infrastructure.
  • Bottled-water sellers, tanker contractors, borehole drillers and filtration installers gain business when residents lose confidence in public supply.
  • Investigations document questionable tanker procurement, but financial incentives alone do not prove sabotage; the 2026 SAHRC inquiry said its evidence was insufficient to conclude that tanker procurement was corrupt.
🫶What it means for us
  • In Edenvale, separate three questions: is water available in the Vaal system, can the network deliver it, and is the water reaching our tap safe?
  • Use notices for the actual suburb and reservoir zone, then read recent local laboratory results alongside the sample date and location.
  • Store a small protected drinking-water reserve separately from water intended for toilets and cleaning, and size any larger tank around measured household use.
  • Choose filtration for a demonstrated problem and a verified model-specific claim; a chlorine-taste filter is not an emergency disinfection system.
  • Check Ekurhuleni's rules before drilling or connecting alternative supplies, and budget for recurring costs rather than treating a tank or borehole as permanent independence.

🧠 Understand it

The basics, in plain words.

1Where your tap water comes from6 key points
  • Edenvale falls under the City of Ekurhuleni. City outage notices list Edenvale among the areas it supplies.
  • The journey starts in Lesotho. The Lesotho Highlands Water Project (a joint Lesotho and South Africa scheme) stores water in the Katse and Mohale dams and sends it through tunnels into the Vaal River system.
  • The Vaal system is a network of 14 linked dams. Rand Water takes its raw water from the Vaal Dam. Sterkfontein Dam is the backup and only tops up the Vaal Dam if it drops below 18%.
  • Rand Water is the bulk supplier. It cleans the water at big plants like Zuikerbosch in Vereeniging: it adds lime so dirt clumps and sinks, filters it through sand, adjusts the pH, then adds chlorine to kill germs.
  • Rand Water makes about 5,000 million litres a day. About 77% goes to Joburg, Tshwane and Ekurhuleni. It pumps into municipal reservoirs, not into homes.
  • Ekurhuleni buys that water and runs the reservoirs, street pipes, meters and billing. Pipes and geysers inside your property are usually the landlord's or body corporate's job.
2Why the taps run dry6 key points
  • Most Gauteng outages are not about empty dams. Experts call it an 'institutional drought': the water exists, but pumps, pipes and power fail.
  • Power cuts stop the pumps. On 2 November 2026 an Ekurhuleni power failure stopped Rand Water's Mapleton pump station, and Rand Water warned of shortages in Ekurhuleni.
  • Planned maintenance causes big cuts. Rand Water's December 2025 maintenance hit Edenvale, and the city warned that pressure can take up to two weeks to recover, especially in high-lying areas.
  • Demand is too high. Rand Water is licensed to take 1,600 million cubic metres a year but took 193 million more than that in 2023/24.
  • Leaks waste a lot. Gauteng loses about 431 billion litres a year. Ekurhuleni does better than most metros here: its losses are within international averages.
  • Help is coming slowly. Phase II of the Lesotho project (Polihali Dam) will raise transfers from 780 million to about 1.27 billion cubic metres a year. Water is expected around 2028 to 2030, and the date has slipped before.
3Is it safe to drink?6 key points
  • SANS 241 is South Africa's drinking water standard. It sets limits for germs, chemicals and things like cloudiness. Germs such as E. coli must be at zero.
  • Blue Drop is the government's audit of drinking water systems. A score above 95% earns a certificate. In the 2023 full audit, Ekurhuleni scored 97% and Joburg 98%. Only 26 systems in the whole country were certified.
  • The 2025 Blue Drop progress check (released March 2026) found big metros 'generally performing well', with Gauteng and the Western Cape strongest. It was a progress check, not a full audit.
  • Ekurhuleni's own 2024/25 report shows 98% to 100% compliance with SANS 241 across all measures.
  • Green Drop audits sewage treatment. In 2026 Ekurhuleni won six Green Drop certificates and took the top three wastewater spots in the country. That matters because treated sewage flows back into rivers.
  • You can check results yourself on the government's IRIS 'My Water' map. By law, a city must warn residents if its water is a health risk. Boil only if a notice tells you to, or if you're using stored or untreated water.
4Filtering it yourself6 key points
  • Start with the goal. Your tap water already meets the standard, so a filter here mostly fixes taste and chlorine smell. The US CDC (its national public health agency) says you may not need one at all if your water is clean.
  • Carbon jug filters (activated carbon traps chemicals on its surface) improve taste and chlorine and can cut some lead. They are not built to remove germs. A Brita Aluna 2.4 L jug is R699 at Yuppiechef, and a 2-pack of Maxtra Pro cartridges is R699, so about R350 per cartridge. Check the box for how often to swap it.
  • Tap-mounted filters screw onto the kitchen tap with no plumbing, which suits a rental. The Brita On Tap system is R1,999 at Yuppiechef.
  • Reverse osmosis (RO) pushes water through a very fine membrane. It removes lead, copper and salt and can cut fluoride and nitrate. It also strips minerals, sends some water down the drain and needs under-sink plumbing. Local 5-stage units start around R1,700.
  • UV filters use light to kill germs but don't remove chemicals. They need power and a pre-filter. They're useful for rainwater, but overkill for treated city water. Ceramic gravity filters trap dirt and many bacteria. They don't stop viruses or dissolved chemicals unless they also have a carbon core.
  • Check certification. NSF/ANSI (a US standards body) labels: 42 means taste and chlorine, 53 means health contaminants like lead, 58 means RO systems, 55 means UV, and 401 means newer contaminants. The numbers are not a ranking. Look up the exact model in NSF's database.
5Backup water and independence6 key points
  • Store at least 4 litres per person per day, for at least 3 days. For the two of you, that's about 24 litres minimum. Aim for about 110 litres, which covers two weeks.
  • Use clean, food-grade containers with tight lids. Never reuse bleach or chemical bottles. Wash them with soap and a weak bleach rinse, label them with the date, keep them cool and dark, and refill every 6 months.
  • A 1,000 L JoJo vertical tank costs about R1,935 at Hinterland. It needs a level, solid base. When full it weighs about a tonne, so don't put it on a balcony without checking.
  • In a rental, the landlord doesn't have to supply a tank, so you'd usually pay. Once installed, a tank counts as a fixture and becomes the landlord's property unless you both sign an agreement that you can take it with you.
  • Under the National Water Act, you can collect and use rainwater off a roof without a licence. In a flat you probably don't control the roof or gutters, so ask the body corporate. Boil or treat rainwater before drinking it.
  • To make water safe, bring it to a rolling boil for 1 minute. The CDC says to boil for 3 minutes only above about 2,000 m, and Edenvale sits around 1,600 m. Unscented bleach works too, but wait 30 minutes and check the strength on the label, because CDC drop counts assume 5 to 9% bleach. Neither method removes chemicals.

🔬 Deep dive

Past the basics: the evidence, the economics, the misconceptions.

1Why full dams can still mean dry taps13 key points
  • The Integrated Vaal River System [linked dams, rivers and transfer schemes] supplies a region much larger than Ekurhuleni.
  • Lesotho Highlands transfers enter South Africa through the Ash River and feed the wider Vaal system; they do not run through a dedicated pipe to an Edenvale kitchen.
  • Katse and Mohale store water in Lesotho, while Sterkfontein provides reserve storage for the Vaal system.
  • The 2024 Lesotho tunnel shutdown was planned maintenance, so its existence alone did not establish a shortage at Johannesburg taps.
  • Rand Water's main treatment sites are Vereeniging and Zuikerbosch, followed by booster stations that push water towards higher ground.
  • Coagulation [making fine particles clump], settling and sand filtration remove suspended material before disinfection.
  • Rand Water also uses chloramination [adding ammonia to chlorine to form a longer-lasting disinfectant] in its distribution process.
  • Electricity failure can stop pumping even when raw water and treatment capacity are available.
  • A municipal reservoir is a short-term buffer, so its usable storage can run down during a prolonged interruption.
  • Recovery requires incoming flow to exceed current demand, which explains why restoring a pump does not instantly refill every reservoir.
  • Higher areas may recover later because delivering water uphill requires enough pressure [force pushing water through pipes].
  • The November 2026 Mapleton outage illustrates dependence on municipal electricity, but the report does not establish that every Edenvale property uses that station.
  • For a local outage, ask which reservoir supplies the address, what failed, whether pumping has resumed and whether reservoir levels are recovering.
2What water scores actually prove14 key points
  • Blue Drop evaluates drinking-water systems, Green Drop evaluates wastewater systems, and No Drop evaluates water-use efficiency and losses.
  • A Green Drop failure means sewage management needs attention; it does not itself show that treated household drinking water failed a test.
  • An audit score combines management and operational evidence, so it is different from the percentage of samples meeting a contaminant limit.
  • The 2023 finding that 46% of systems had poor or bad microbiological compliance describes systems and an earlier sampling period, not 46% of all glasses of water today.
  • Read the assessment year, sampling period and release date separately; the 2025 Green Drop report released in 2026 assessed the 2023/24 municipal year.
  • SANS 241:2025 strengthens risk management, monitoring and incident response, while historical datasets can still identify SANS 241:2015 as their reporting basis.
  • E. coli [a bacterium used as an indicator of faecal contamination] is a warning signal, not a test for every possible pathogen.
  • A TDS meter [a meter estimating total dissolved solids from conductivity] cannot establish whether water contains harmful bacteria or every toxic chemical.
  • Taste, smell and clarity matter for acceptability, but clear water can still contain germs and an unfamiliar taste does not identify the cause.
  • Request the sample location, date, result, units, applicable limit and laboratory method rather than accepting an undated claim of SANS compliance.
  • Use a laboratory whose accreditation covers the relevant water tests, and follow its bottle, transport and holding-time instructions.
  • A passing sample before a storage tank does not establish the quality after that tank or after an overdue household filter.
  • Low or negative pressure can draw contamination through leaks or unsafe cross-connections [links between drinking and non-drinking pipes].
  • An international review linked outages with gastrointestinal illness, but its pooled estimates cannot calculate this household's risk or explain an individual pregnancy loss.
3Buy treatment for the actual hazard15 key points
  • Activated carbon works mainly by adsorption [substances sticking to a large internal surface], and its performance depends on the material, contact time and cartridge condition.
  • A jug sold for chlorine taste should not be assumed to remove bacteria, nitrate, arsenic or all metals.
  • Some certified carbon systems reduce lead or particular PFAS [persistent industrial chemicals], so claims that carbon can never remove them are also misleading.
  • Chloramine reduction is a separate performance question from ordinary chlorine reduction, which matters when checking equipment for Rand Water supply.
  • RO [reverse osmosis] forces water through a membrane to reduce many dissolved substances, but the complete system still needs the right certification and maintenance.
  • RO also produces concentrate [water carrying rejected substances], so compare litres rejected per litre delivered at the actual operating pressure.
  • UV [ultraviolet light] inactivates germs when the delivered dose is adequate; it does not remove dissolved chemical pollution.
  • Cloudiness, lamp ageing, dirty sleeves and excessive flow can undermine UV performance, so prefiltration and a working failure alarm matter.
  • NSF/ANSI 42 concerns aesthetic claims [taste, odour and similar qualities], while 53 concerns specified health-related reductions and 58 covers RO systems.
  • NSF/ANSI 55 distinguishes Class A disinfection systems from Class B systems used for supplemental treatment of already disinfected water.
  • A standard number is not a universal contaminant guarantee; check the exact system, cartridge, reduction claim, rated flow and service life in a certifier's listing.
  • A point-of-use system [treatment at one drinking tap] can keep operating costs lower when the identified issue concerns drinking and cooking water.
  • Removing disinfectant before long storage or a whole-house pipe network creates a new maintenance responsibility because the residual protection is reduced.
  • Compare five-year ownership costs in Rands, including installation, cartridges, electricity, laboratory checks, sanitation and RO reject water.
  • During a boil-water advisory, follow the advisory even if a household jug is installed; boiling cannot make chemically contaminated water safe.
4Storage, rain and groundwater are different systems16 key points
  • A municipal backup tank stores previously treated water, while a rainwater tank collects a new raw-water source with different contamination risks.
  • CDC's emergency baseline is about 3.8 litres per person daily for three days, covering drinking and limited essential uses rather than normal showers and laundry.
  • For two people, that baseline is about 23 litres for three days; this is a calculation, and heat, illness or other needs can increase it.
  • Estimate larger-tank endurance as usable litres divided by measured daily demand; 1,000 usable litres at 200 litres per day lasts five days by calculation.
  • A full 1,000-litre tank holds roughly one tonne of water before the tank's own weight, so the base and supporting structure matter.
  • Use protected openings, suitable potable-water materials and a cleaning plan, and prevent a booster pump from creating an unsafe connection to the municipal main.
  • Keep stored drinking water in clean, closed containers and avoid dipping hands or cups into the supply.
  • CDC advises replacing self-filled emergency container water every six months; this is separate from managing a permanently plumbed tank.
  • Rainwater yield depends on roof area, rainfall and collection losses; 1 mm of rain on 1 square metre represents 1 litre before losses.
  • A first-flush diverter [a device discarding the first dirty roof runoff] reduces contamination but does not establish drinking-water safety.
  • The South African rooftop study found microbial contamination despite generally acceptable chemical results, so a good chemical score was not enough.
  • National Schedule 1 allows storage and use of roof runoff, but municipal rules still govern safe plumbing and drinking use.
  • Section 70(2) of Ekurhuleni's published amended bylaw prohibits using water collected in rainwater tanks as potable water [water for drinking].
  • Ekurhuleni's published rules require prior approval for new boreholes and prohibit sinking them in dolomite areas [rock terrain vulnerable to ground instability].
  • A national Schedule 1 household water use does not remove municipal drilling requirements, and a higher-volume or commercial use may need a different national authorisation.
  • A borehole's pumping rate does not prove a sustainable long-term supply or safe chemistry, and groundwater used indoors can still attract sewer charges.
5Follow the money without inventing a conspiracy14 key points
  • Non-revenue water [supplied water that does not generate billed revenue] includes physical leaks, meter and accounting errors, and authorised unbilled use.
  • Unpaid bills are a related cash-collection problem, but technically billed water does not become non-revenue water merely because a customer has not paid.
  • Several public summaries blur those categories, so ask for the utility's water balance [an accounting of where supplied water goes].
  • Fixing a real leak saves treated water and pumping costs, while improving a faulty meter can raise revenue without saving the same physical volume.
  • The municipality pays upstream for treated water even when a local leak prevents it from reaching a paying customer.
  • Ekurhuleni announced an 11% water-tariff increase and an 8.35% sanitation increase from 1 July 2026 after consultation.
  • The earlier draft proposed a larger water increase, so a draft budget PDF is not evidence of the final household tariff.
  • A block tariff [a different price for each usage band] requires adding the charge for each band rather than pricing the whole month's use at the highest band reached.
  • A useful bill comparison separates consumption, fixed charges, VAT, sanitation and any qualifying household subsidy.
  • The Lesotho treaty shares a calculated benefit from the transfer scheme through royalties; it is more complex than buying every litre at a single shop price.
  • People losing land or grazing access in Lesotho can bear costs even when national royalties rise, which is why compensation reporting belongs beside engineering progress.
  • A lucrative tanker contract is a reason to inspect procurement, delivery records and conflicts of interest, not proof that a specific pipe was deliberately broken.
  • Compare investigative allegations, court findings, official replies and later appeal outcomes instead of treating them as the same kind of evidence.
  • For accountability, track repair completion, water losses, reservoir recovery and current test results alongside money spent; a new committee or dashboard is not a repaired pipe.

❓ Questions to ask

Screenshot these or read them out.

  1. Landlord: Can we fit a tap-mounted or under-sink filter? Can we have that in writing, including who removes it and who fixes any damage when we leave?
  2. Landlord or body corporate: Does the building have its own storage tank or booster pump for outages? Who owns the pipes between the meter and our flat?
  3. Body corporate: What are the rules on placing a water tank, and what weight can a balcony or yard take?
  4. Ekurhuleni (0860 543 000): Which reservoir supplies our street? Is our area high-lying, which means slower recovery after outages?
  5. Filter seller: Which NSF/ANSI standard is this model certified to, for which contaminants? Can I see the listing?
  6. Filter seller: How long does a cartridge last in litres or months, what does a replacement cost, and how much water does the RO unit send down the drain?

⚠️ Watch out for

Myths, traps and red flags.

  • TDS meter and 'electrolysis' demos where tap water turns brown are sales tricks. TDS (total dissolved solids) measures minerals, not safety.
  • 'Tested to NSF standards' is not the same as 'NSF certified'. Ask for the listing.
  • Alkaline and 'structured' water health claims aren't backed by solid evidence. Don't pay extra for them.
  • An old cartridge is worse than none. It stops working and can grow bacteria, so change it on schedule.
  • A full Vaal Dam doesn't mean your taps will run. Most Gauteng outages are about pumps, power and pipes.
  • Boiling kills germs but does nothing for fuel or chemical contamination.
  • Don't store drinking water in old bleach, pesticide or chemical containers, or in glass that can break.
