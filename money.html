Money

Money follows a few simple rules: interest, inflation and fees. Learn those, and banks, car dealers and influencers lose their edge over you. Everything here uses South African figures checked in November 2026.

🧭 The system

The same four questions for every track. Follow the money, then decide.

💸Who pays
  • Households pay banks through loan interest, account charges and payment fees.
  • Investors pay fund managers, platforms and advisers through charges that often come straight out of their investments.
  • Workers and employers fund retirement contributions, while workers bear the cost of early withdrawals and weak investment returns.
  • Taxpayers fund housing subsidies and public services through taxes collected by SARS [South African Revenue Service].
  • Member banks fund CODI [Corporation for Deposit Insurance], which protects qualifying deposits within its coverage limit.
⚖️Who decides
  • Parliament makes tax and credit laws, while SARS administers taxes and Treasury develops financial policy.
  • SARB [South African Reserve Bank] sets monetary policy through its Monetary Policy Committee, while banks set individual lending offers.
  • The Prudential Authority supervises financial institutions' safety, while the FSCA [Financial Sector Conduct Authority] regulates market conduct.
  • The NCR [National Credit Regulator] enforces the NCA [National Credit Act] and registers lenders, credit bureaus and debt counsellors.
  • Lenders decide credit applications using their own rules and bureau information, while retirement fund rules determine members' investment and withdrawal options.
🏦Who profits
  • Banks earn net interest income [interest received minus interest paid] and income from fees and other services.
  • Shareholders receive dividends and changes in share value, including pension funds that own bank shares.
  • Fund managers and platforms can collect charges even when an investor loses money.
  • Advisers may earn product commissions, initial fees or continuing charges based on the assets they advise on.
  • Credit bureaus sell information services, while property transactions generate business for estate agents, conveyancers and home-loan providers.
🫶What it means for us
  • Build the household plan around essential expenses, available cash and goals before choosing products.
  • Compare the total rand cost and exit conditions of each offer, including every investment fee and any final loan balloon.
  • Keep separate records of Sean's and Mishka's tax-free contributions because the limits belong to individuals.
  • Use regulators' registers and complaint routes to challenge bad information or treatment without assuming a licence guarantees a good product.
  • Treat the dated rules here as checked on 15 November 2026 and revisit official sources before a tax filing, withdrawal or major purchase.

🧠 Understand it

The basics, in plain words.

1How money moves6 key points
  • Interest is the price of borrowing money. When you borrow, you pay it. When you save, you earn it.
  • Compound interest means you earn interest on your interest. R1,000 a month at 10% a year grows to about R759,000 after 20 years. You only put in R240,000.
  • Inflation (prices rising over time) quietly eats cash. Money sitting in a 0% account loses buying power every year.
  • Banks make money on the spread (the gap between what they pay savers and what they charge borrowers), plus fees.
  • The repo rate is the rate the Reserve Bank (SARB) charges banks. Prime is the base rate banks lend at, normally repo plus 3.5 points.
  • In September 2026 the SARB raised repo to 7.25%, so prime went to 10.75%. Most home and car loans are priced as prime plus or minus something, so your instalment moves when the SARB moves.
2Debt: friend or trap6 key points
  • Good debt buys something that grows or earns, like a home or a skill. Bad debt buys things that lose value fast, like clothes on store credit.
  • Your credit score is a number bureaus (companies that keep your borrowing history) give you. It drives the interest rate a bank offers you.
  • By law you get a free credit report from each credit bureau every year. TransUnion and Experian both offer this online.
  • If your report has a mistake, you can dispute it with the bureau that issued it, for free.
  • Paying on time every month matters most. Keep credit card and store card balances low compared to their limits.
  • The National Credit Act (NCA) is the law that governs lending. Only borrow from lenders registered with the National Credit Regulator (NCR).
3Growing money6 key points
  • Step one is an emergency fund (cash for surprises, like a job loss or a car repair). Aim for 3 to 6 months of expenses in a separate savings account.
  • A Tax-Free Savings Account (TFSA) lets your money grow with no tax on interest, dividends or growth. From 1 March 2026 the limit is R46,000 a year and R500,000 in your lifetime, per person.
  • Go over the TFSA limit and SARS charges 40% tax on the excess. Unused yearly space is lost, it does not carry over.
  • A retirement annuity (RA, a personal retirement fund) is tax-deductible. You can deduct up to 27.5% of your income, capped at R430,000 a year from March 2026.
  • Under the two-pot system (since September 2024), one-third of new retirement contributions goes to a savings pot. You can withdraw from it once per tax year, minimum R2,000, taxed at your marginal rate (the tax rate on your top slice of income).
  • Low-cost index funds and ETFs (exchange-traded funds, which buy a whole market in one go) beat most expensive funds over time. Fees matter: R1,000 a month for 30 years grows to about R1.83m at 9% a year, but only R1.35m at 7.5%.
4Housing: rent vs buy6 key points
  • Renting is not 'dead money'. A bond also has dead money: interest, rates, levies, insurance and repairs.
  • A R1.2m bond over 20 years at prime (10.75%) costs about R12,180 a month. Compare that full cost to your rent, not just the price tag.
  • Transfer duty (a tax on buying property) is 0% on homes up to R1,210,000 for 2026/27. You still pay bond registration and conveyancing (legal transfer) fees.
  • A deposit lowers your loan and often gets you a better rate. Banks check your income, expenses, credit record and existing debts.
  • Bond originators like ooba and BetterBond send one application to several banks so they compete for you. ooba's service is free to you.
  • First Home Finance (formerly FLISP) is a once-off government subsidy for first-time buyers with household income of R3,501 to R22,000 a month. If you earn above that together, you will not qualify.
5Cars and car loans6 key points
  • A car loses value from day one. Treat it as a cost, not an investment.
  • Vehicle finance uses the car as security, so it is usually cheaper than an unsecured personal loan.
  • Small rate gaps add up. A R250,000 car over 72 months costs about R4,730 a month at 10.75%, and about R4,990 at 12.75%. That gap is roughly R19,000 over the loan.
  • A balloon payment (a big lump sum you owe at the end) lowers the monthly instalment but costs more. With a 30% balloon at 12.75%, the instalment drops to about R4,290, but the total rises to about R384,000.
  • Total cost of ownership is instalment plus insurance, fuel, services, tyres and tolls. Budget for all of it before you choose a car.
  • A bigger deposit, a shorter term, no balloon and a clean credit report are what get you a better deal by Q2 2027.
6Building your own thing6 key points
  • Side income is taxable. SARS counts it together with your salary.
  • For 2026/27, you pay no income tax under 65 if your total taxable income is below R99,000.
  • If you earn income that is not salary, SARS treats you as a provisional taxpayer. That means you pay tax in advance, by the end of August and the end of February.
  • Keep records of what you earn and spend on the side hustle. Legitimate business costs can reduce the tax you owe.
  • You can register a company online with CIPC (the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission). Start as a sole trader first if it is small, and register a company once it grows.
  • Grow skills and income first. A raise or a client is worth far more than chasing a few extra percent on savings.

🔬 Deep dive

Past the basics: the evidence, the economics, the misconceptions.

1Money is a promise, and banks have constraints14 key points
  • A bank deposit is the bank's debt to you, while physical cash is central bank money.
  • SARB's 2025 money background note explains how bank lending creates matching loans and deposits.
  • A bank records a loan as an asset [money owed to it] and a deposit as a liability [money it owes].
  • Creating deposits does not give banks unlimited spending power because lending brings funding needs, credit losses and regulatory constraints.
  • Bank profits must be assessed after funding costs, operating costs and bad debts rather than treating all interest receipts as profit.
  • BusinessTech's bank comparison mixes different financial year ends, so its figures cannot be read as one identical reporting period.
  • The repo rate [SARB's policy interest rate] influences borrowing costs but does not determine the exact rate on your loan.
  • SARB now targets 3% inflation with a tolerance band of one percentage point on either side.
  • SARB says policy changes typically affect the economy over roughly 12 to 24 months.
  • Lower inflation means prices rise more slowly rather than returning to their old level.
  • CODI covers up to R100,000 per qualifying depositor per bank across qualifying deposits, including interest.
  • Several accounts at the same bank share that deposit-insurance limit.
  • A bank money-market deposit can qualify for cover, while a money-market unit trust is an investment outside CODI's cover.
  • A return is real [adjusted for inflation] only after accounting for the loss of buying power.
2Credit is a contract, not a character score15 key points
  • A bureau supplies credit information, while the lender makes the approval decision.
  • A high credit score cannot make a loan affordable if repayments leave too little for living costs.
  • TransUnion's page explains that errors and identity theft can affect the report used by a lender.
  • The NCR's dispute guidance requires a bureau investigation before escalation to the regulator.
  • Keep the dispute reference and payment evidence so the complaint can be traced.
  • A quoted monthly instalment hides the loan term, interest, service fees, insurance and any final balance.
  • A balloon [a lump sum postponed until the end] reduces monthly capital repayment without forgiving the postponed debt.
  • Interest continues on the outstanding balloon balance during the loan.
  • Negative equity [owing more than the car can sell for] makes changing cars expensive.
  • A deposit and a shorter repayment term reduce debt exposure but must leave enough cash for emergencies.
  • Debt consolidation replaces debts with a new loan, while debt counselling restructures repayments through the NCA process.
  • Debt counselling does not cancel debt and usually prevents further borrowing while the process applies.
  • A debt counsellor's own NCR registration must be checked even when the service uses a familiar company name.
  • GroundUp's historical grant-lending analysis shows how a legally permitted price can still be harmful for a vulnerable household.
  • Stellenbosch Law Clinic's payroll investigation shows why deductions deserve scrutiny even when an employer processes them.
3The investment and its tax wrapper are different choices17 key points
  • A TFSA [tax-free savings account] is a tax treatment, while an ETF [exchange-traded fund] is an investment that may fit inside it.
  • For the 2027 tax year from 1 March 2026 to 28 February 2027, SARS lists the annual TFSA contribution limit as R46,000 per person.
  • The TFSA lifetime contribution limit remains R500,000 per person across providers.
  • Investment growth inside a TFSA does not use contribution room.
  • Withdrawing TFSA money does not restore contribution room when that money is paid back.
  • Use a formal provider transfer to move a TFSA instead of withdrawing and depositing the cash yourself.
  • An RA [retirement annuity] offers contribution tax deductions in exchange for retirement access restrictions.
  • SARS lists a 2026/27 deduction ceiling of 27.5% of the greater of remuneration or taxable income, subject to the R430,000 annual cap and statutory calculation rules.
  • That deduction limit covers pension, provident and RA contributions together.
  • A tax deduction reduces taxable income rather than refunding the entire amount invested.
  • Retirement withdrawals and retirement income can be taxed later, so an RA is not a promise that all future benefits are tax-free.
  • Regulation 28 [retirement fund investment rules] limits portfolio exposures, so an RA has different investment constraints from a discretionary account.
  • Under two-pot rules, generally one-third of new contributions goes to the savings component and two-thirds to the retirement component.
  • Older savings normally remain in a vested component [money governed by earlier access rules], so many people actually have three components.
  • The savings component permits a minimum R2,000 withdrawal generally once per tax year per fund, subject to eligibility and fund rules.
  • A two-pot withdrawal adds taxable income and can lose both administration charges and future compound growth.
  • Available cash, expected tax relief and time until the goal should determine the choice between ordinary savings, a TFSA and retirement saving.
4Compounding rewards patience and also compounds fees17 key points
  • An index [a rule-based market measure] is a benchmark rather than something you can buy directly.
  • An index fund aims to track an index through an investable portfolio.
  • An ETF can hold shares, bonds or other exposures, so the ETF label alone does not tell you its risk.
  • A global equity fund bought in rands still carries foreign-market and currency exposure.
  • Several funds can hold the same large companies, so counting fund names does not measure diversification [spreading exposure].
  • SPIVA's year-end 2025 study found 94% of South African equity funds lagged its capped composite benchmark over ten years.
  • The same study found only 32% of short-term bond funds lagged their benchmark over ten years, so active management results differ by category.
  • Survivorship bias [ignoring funds that closed or merged] can make investment results look better than investors actually experienced.
  • A benchmark return is not the return of a purchasable ETF after its fees and trading costs.
  • The TER [total expense ratio] does not capture every cost an investor pays.
  • EAC [effective annual cost] estimates the combined effect of investment, advice, administration and other charges over stated periods.
  • Ask for EAC figures for the same contribution amount and holding period before comparing products.
  • Illustrative arithmetic: R100,000 growing at 8% a year for 30 years becomes about R1,006,266 before tax.
  • Illustrative arithmetic: reducing that annual growth to 6% leaves about R574,349 after 30 years.
  • Those calculations assume constant annual returns and no further contributions rather than predicting market performance.
  • Ask an adviser for the rand cost, payment model, conflicts and work delivered before accepting a recurring percentage fee.
  • An FSCA licence must match the firm, person and service being offered because scammers can copy genuine licence numbers.
5Housing and business decisions are cash-flow decisions21 key points
  • Rent buys housing services, while buying adds a concentrated property investment financed by a long debt contract.
  • Compare rent with interest, rates, levies, insurance, maintenance and transaction costs rather than with the bond instalment alone.
  • Capital repayment [reducing the loan balance] builds equity rather than disappearing as an expense.
  • Buying also ties up a deposit that could otherwise earn an investment return.
  • Daily Maverick's suggested eight-year holding period is a planning rule of thumb rather than a universal break-even date.
  • Compare actual Edenvale sale prices and rents for similar homes because Cape Town price growth is not a Johannesburg forecast.
  • Check sectional-title body corporate accounts and maintenance plans for possible special levies [extra charges to owners].
  • Ekurhuleni publishes separate rates, electricity, water, sewerage and waste schedules for the 2026/27 municipal year.
  • Illustrative arithmetic: a R1 million loan over 20 years at a constant 10% costs about R9,650 a month before fees and insurance.
  • Illustrative arithmetic: the same loan at a constant 12% costs about R11,011 a month before fees and insurance.
  • The 10% example repays about R1.316 million in interest over 20 years if payments and the rate never change.
  • A longer loan can lower the instalment while increasing total interest.
  • A side business should track sales, costs and unpaid invoices separately from household spending.
  • A sole proprietor [an individual trading in their own capacity] declares business income on their personal tax return and carries personal liability.
  • Private spending does not become a business deduction simply because it is paid from a business account.
  • SARS generally requires deductible business expenditure to be incurred for trade and producing income, with separate rules for capital assets.
  • Provisional tax [advance income-tax payments] can apply to side-business income even when PAYE [tax withheld from salary] already covers a day job.
  • A company adds CIPC annual-return and beneficial-ownership duties alongside its SARS obligations.
  • The SARS Budget 2026 FAQ says it administers the R2.3 million compulsory VAT threshold from 1 April 2026 while noting the legislative process.
  • Use the VAT threshold for taxable supplies [sales counted under VAT rules] rather than treating it as a profit threshold.
  • Set aside tax from business receipts before treating the remaining cash as available household income.

❓ Questions to ask

Screenshot these or read them out.

  1. What is the interest rate as prime plus or minus, and is it fixed or linked to prime?
  2. What is the total amount I will repay over the full term, including all fees?
  3. Is there a balloon or residual payment in this deal? Show me the quote without one.
  4. What is the monthly service fee and initiation fee, and are they included in the instalment?
  5. What credit life or insurance are you adding, and can I use my own cover instead?
  6. What are the total annual fees on this fund (TER plus platform plus advice fees)?
  7. Are you registered with the FSCA, and what is your FSP number?

⚠️ Watch out for

Myths, traps and red flags.

  • Forex and crypto 'mentors' selling courses or signals. Most earn from your fees and sign-ups, not from trading.
  • Guaranteed high returns are a lie. Real investments go up and down.
  • MLMs (multi-level marketing) where you profit by recruiting people, not by selling to real customers.
  • Deepfake videos of Ramaphosa, Motsepe or TV presenters promoting investments. The FSCA has warned about these.
  • Loan sharks (mashonisas) who want to keep your bank card or ID as security. Walk away.
  • High balloon payments that make a car look affordable. The lump sum does not go away.
  • Two-pot withdrawals for non-emergencies. You pay tax now and lose years of compound growth.
  • Paying a website for your credit report. You get one free from each bureau every year.

🧠 Breakdown · 6 key points
  • The 57-page note distinguishes central bank cash from commercial bank demand deposits.
  • Commercial bank deposits are liabilities of the bank and are created mainly through credit extension.
  • A loan appears as a bank asset and the matching deposit as a bank liability.
  • Deposits can also arise when cash is deposited or a bank buys a financial asset.
  • A CBDC [central bank digital currency] would differ from an ordinary banking-app balance because it would be central bank money.
  • The note examines cash access and payment inclusion as well as technical experiments, rather than presenting new technology alone as a reason to issue a CBDC.

WebsiteProvisional taxSouth African Revenue Service · Current guidance, checked 2026-11-15

Essential for a salaried person who begins earning freelance or business income.

🧠 Breakdown · 6 key points
  • Provisional tax is a way to pay income tax in advance, not an additional tax on top of income tax.
  • It generally requires two estimated payments during the tax year and allows an optional third payment afterwards.
  • Payments are credited against the final assessed income-tax liability.
  • Business income can bring an individual into the system, while statutory exclusions apply to some non-business income.
  • Companies automatically fall within the provisional-tax system.
  • Taxpayers must determine their own status and request the IRP6 [provisional tax return] through eFiling.
