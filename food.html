Food

You can buy most of your fresh food without a supermarket. Edenvale has its own farmers market, and two big wholesale markets (City Deep and Springs) are open to the public. A few pots on a ground-floor patio can cover your herbs and greens most of the year.

🧭 The system

The same four questions for every track. Follow the money, then decide.

💸Who pays
  • Households pay for farming, processing, transport, cooling, spoilage and selling through the food price.
  • Farmers pay for seed, fertiliser, water, labour and transport before knowing what a perishable harvest will earn.
  • Small traders carry spoilage and transport costs even when customers cannot afford a price increase.
  • PMBEJD put its October 2026 household food basket at R5,512.53, while its basic nutritional basket for seven people cost R6,692.25.
  • Those baskets describe larger low-income households, so neither is a ready-made budget for Sean and Mishka.
⚖️Who decides
  • Farmers choose crops, but access to land, water, finance and buyers constrains those choices.
  • Municipalities manage public fresh produce market infrastructure, while agents sell farmers’ consignments [produce sent for sale on the farmer’s behalf].
  • Large buyers set purchasing specifications, delivery requirements and contract terms that small farmers may struggle to meet.
  • The Competition Commission investigates barriers to competition; the Department of Health sets food labelling rules.
  • R3337 warning-label rules were still reported as draft in October 2026, so proposals must be distinguished from enforceable requirements.
🏦Who profits
  • Input suppliers, transporters, processors, market agents, wholesalers and sellers each receive part of household food spending.
  • Revenue is money received, while profit is what remains after costs, so a large farm-to-consumer price gap is not a profit estimate.
  • The 2025 fresh produce inquiry found competition problems but made no conclusive finding that retailers were profiteering.
  • Farm-direct businesses can retain more of the selling price, but must also fund packing, deliveries and customer service.
  • Informal traders earn livelihoods by splitting bulk purchases into affordable quantities and bringing food closer to customers.
🫶What it means for us
  • Start with Edenvale’s produce market and compare it with City Deep’s small-quantity traders before committing to bulk trips.
  • Compare the cost per edible kilogram, including fuel, delivery, packaging and food that spoils.
  • Use delivery pages to check Edenvale coverage; a Johannesburg address alone does not establish that a farm delivers to your suburb.
  • Grow a few herbs and leafy crops first, then expand only when the light, water, harvest and costs justify it.

🧠 Understand it

The basics, in plain words.

1How food reaches Joburg6 key points
  • Most fresh fruit and veg goes through council-run wholesale markets. Farmers send produce there, agents sell it for them, and buyers pay the day's price.
  • Joburg Market in City Deep is the biggest of these. It calls itself the leading fresh produce market in Africa.
  • Prices change every day with supply. Good rains in 2026 brought more produce into the Springs market and pushed some prices down.
  • Quick context on supermarkets: four chains run formal grocery retail. In 2019 the Competition Commission found that their exclusive leases in malls kept smaller shops out. That's why buying around them takes a bit of effort.
  • Food is expensive right now. PMBEJD (a Pietermaritzburg research group) says its Joburg household food basket cost R5,766.71 in October 2026.
  • No VAT is charged on 21 basic foods, including brown bread, maize meal, eggs, dried beans, and fresh fruit and veg. Buying these loose and direct gets you the most value.
2Buying direct near Edenvale6 key points
  • Edenvale Farmers Market runs outside De Backery in the Edenvale CBD. It's on the first and last Saturday of each month, 07:00 to 12:00. Stalls sell veg, fruit, eggs and meat.
  • Anyone can buy at Joburg Market. Bring your ID and register at a customer service kiosk for a free buyer card. Load money onto it at the cashier, then buy from the agents in the halls.
  • For smaller amounts at Joburg Market, use the People's Market (the old Mandela Market). Traders there split bulk stock into smaller lots, and it's cash only.
  • The Ekurhuleni Fresh Produce Market in Springs is closer to home and open to everyone. You don't need to be a business. A market agent says prices are often better than in shops.
  • Bryanston Organic & Natural Market (40 Culross Road) has been running for about 40 years. Go there for certified-organic and specialty produce. It runs on Thursdays and Saturdays.
  • For meat, buy a whole lamb or a quarter beef from a farm or a butcher who buys direct, and have it cut and packed for the freezer. Ask where the animal came from and how it was raised.
3Veg boxes, co-ops and bulk buying5 key points
  • Delivery services cut out the supermarket too. Munching Mongoose delivers organic produce, grass-fed meat and dairy across greater Joburg, with no maximum order size.
  • Split a market haul with one or two other households. A pocket of onions or a box of tomatoes is cheap per kg but too much for two people.
  • Buy dry staples like rice, maize meal, lentils, oil and flour in bulk from a cash-and-carry. Always compare the price per kg, not the pack price.
  • Buy what's in season and cheap, then freeze it, sauce it or pickle it. A small chest freezer pays for itself if you buy meat in bulk.
  • Go early. Wholesale markets close by 10:00 or 11:00, and the best stock goes first.
4Growing your own in a rental6 key points
  • Grow in things you can move: grow bags, big pots, or a raised bed on legs. You keep the garden when you move, and the landlord's lawn stays untouched.
  • Johannesburg's last frost is usually mid-August and the first frost around mid-June. Plant tomatoes outside in October or November.
  • Start with easy, high-yield crops: Swiss chard (local 'spinach'), lettuce, radish, cherry tomatoes, bush beans, spring onions, basil and parsley. Give mint its own pot.
  • Give pots at least 6 hours of direct sun. Use bagged potting mix, not garden soil, and make sure water drains out the bottom.
  • Save water: mulch the soil, water early in the morning, and catch rain in a bucket during summer storms. Highveld winters are dry, so pots dry out fast.
  • A worm farm on the patio turns kitchen scraps into free fertiliser. A ready-made one with 500 worms costs about R2,700, or you can build one from stacked crates.
5Labels and ultra-processed food5 key points
  • Ingredients are listed from most to least by weight, so the first three tell you what you're really eating.
  • Ultra-processed food (UPF) is made with industrial ingredients you wouldn't cook with at home, like emulsifiers, flavourings, colourants and sweeteners. Fizzy drinks, packaged snacks and sugary cereals are typical examples.
  • A 2024 BMJ review covered almost 10 million people. It linked eating more UPF with 32 health harms, including heart disease deaths and type 2 diabetes. These studies show links, not proof of cause, but the pattern is consistent.
  • SA's planned front-of-pack warning labels (draft regulation R3337) were still not law in mid-2026. So turn the pack over and read the back.
  • Use the 'per 100g' column to compare products. Check sugar and sodium first.

🔬 Deep dive

Past the basics: the evidence, the economics, the misconceptions.

1Follow the price, then follow the power9 key points
  • A commission market sells produce on a farmer’s behalf, whereas a wholesaler buys stock and resells it.
  • The fresh produce inquiry reported agent commissions commonly around 5% to 7.5%, with weaker bargaining power for small farmers.
  • Municipal market fees and agent commissions pay for different functions and should not be treated as the same charge.
  • A mark-up [increase over the buying price] differs from a margin [share of sales left after a defined set of costs].
  • For illustration, buying at R10 and selling at R15 gives a 50% mark-up and a 33.3% gross margin before operating costs.
  • NAMC’s farm-to-retail spread includes processing and distribution, so it cannot identify one firm’s net profit.
  • NAMC says many market problems identified in 2025 were already raised in its 2006 investigation.
  • Historical exclusion remains visible in barriers to land, water rights, certification and market access for black and emerging farmers.
  • Treat a remedy in an inquiry report as a proposed or ordered change whose implementation still needs evidence.
2Buy together without buying waste11 key points
  • City Deep’s trading halls and its cash-based People’s and Unity markets are different purchasing routes.
  • For two people, loose purchases may cost less overall than a cheaper bulk sack that partly spoils.
  • A buying club [households pooling an order] can split sacks, transport costs and delivery charges.
  • Agree who orders, who pays, how produce is weighed and how shortages are shared before collecting money.
  • A co-operative [member-owned business] has ownership and voting rules; a supplier calling a box a stokvel basket does not prove co-operative ownership.
  • CSA [community supported agriculture] usually involves an ongoing commitment that shares some farm risk, whereas an ordinary veg box may simply be a recurring retail order.
  • Ask whether a subscription specifies the farm, seasonal substitutions, cancellation terms and what happens after a crop failure.
  • No active Edenvale consumer-owned food co-operative was verified in this research, so an informal buying club is a practical experiment rather than an advertised local service.
  • Farm-direct meat needs a clear farm source, inspected slaughter, traceable batches and a reliable cold chain [continuous temperature control].
  • Compare usable cuts and bone content before comparing a mixed meat box with the per-kilogram price of a single cut.
  • Delivery fees and schedules quoted here were visible during research on 15 November 2026 and need confirmation before ordering.
3Build a Highveld growing experiment12 key points
  • Edenvale has summer rainfall and winter frost, so Cape Town’s winter-rainfall calendar does not transfer directly.
  • A USDA hardiness zone [band based on extreme winter cold] cannot specify a safe transplanting date for every garden.
  • Fryd gives mid-August as Johannesburg’s last frost, but that broad estimate should not override a local forecast or the conditions in your yard.
  • Record direct sunlight at the proposed growing spot before buying containers.
  • Start with food-safe containers with drainage and a suitable growing mix.
  • Use deeper containers for larger root systems and support tall plants before they become heavy.
  • In November, beans, basil and summer fruiting crops are options where conditions suit them, but each crop still needs its own sowing and spacing guidance.
  • Try chard, salad leaves and herbs as repeat harvests rather than expecting a few pots to replace staple calories.
  • Sow small batches at intervals to spread the harvest instead of producing one glut.
  • Containers can dry quickly in heat and wind even during the rainy season.
  • Keep a harvest log with grams eaten, water used and money spent on containers, growing mix and seed.
  • Treat home growing as partial resilience and skill-building until your own harvest data demonstrate savings.
4Close loops without garden folklore13 key points
  • Compost is decomposed organic material, not simply freshly chopped kitchen waste.
  • Ordinary compost needs air, moisture, carbon-rich browns and nitrogen-rich greens.
  • A small cold heap should not be assumed to kill pathogens or weed seeds.
  • Worm farms use composting worms and moist bedding, with feeding adjusted to what the worms actually consume.
  • An electric food dehydrator reduces scraps but does not make finished compost.
  • Open-pollinated seed [a stable variety reproduced through ordinary pollination] is useful for seed saving when crossing is controlled.
  • F1 hybrids [first-generation crosses] can produce viable seed, but their offspring may differ substantially from the parent crop.
  • Hybrid does not mean genetically modified, and non-GMO does not establish superior nutrition.
  • Beans and tomatoes are easier starting points for seed saving than crops needing large populations or extensive isolation.
  • Dry seed thoroughly and label the variety and harvest date before storing it cool and dry.
  • IPM [integrated pest management] starts with identifying damage and monitoring pests before choosing a treatment.
  • Water jets and conserving predators can help with aphids, while broad-spectrum sprays can also kill beneficial insects.
  • Organic certification describes a production standard and assurance process, not a guarantee that food is pathogen-free or nutritionally superior.
5Read food claims as evidence claims11 key points
  • NOVA [a classification by the nature and purpose of processing] separates ultra-processed formulations from simpler processed foods.
  • Freezing, pasteurising and canning are forms of processing, so processed does not automatically mean ultra-processed.
  • The BMJ umbrella review [review of existing reviews] found many associations, but observational data cannot eliminate confounding [other differences between groups].
  • Relative risk [risk compared with another group] does not give your personal absolute chance of an illness.
  • Hall’s controlled feeding trial supports a short-term causal effect of the tested diets on intake and weight, but it does not isolate one additive or manufacturing step.
  • Avoid turning evidence about an overall dietary pattern into a claim that every packaged item is poisonous.
  • R146 is the existing labelling framework referenced by the Department of Health, while R3337 contains proposed replacement rules.
  • A legal warning label concerns specified nutrient thresholds or sweeteners, while NOVA concerns processing, so the two systems answer different questions.
  • Current reporting on R3337 describes regulatory delay and unequal access to discussions, which is evidence of governance problems rather than proof of a secret conspiracy.
  • PMBEJD’s household basket and basic nutritional basket measure different questions, while a national inflation average can conceal sharp increases in one food.
  • Use primary reports to test a dramatic headline before deciding who is benefiting or what you should change.

❓ Questions to ask

Screenshot these or read them out.

  1. What's in season and cheapest this week?
  2. Where is your farm, and did you grow this or buy it in?
  3. Do you spray? If so, with what?
  4. Can I get a better price for a full box, pocket or crate?
  5. For meat: how was the animal raised (grass-fed or feedlot), and can you cut and pack a quarter or half for the freezer?
  6. Do you deliver to Edenvale, or is there a pickup point nearby?

⚠️ Watch out for

Myths, traps and red flags.

  • 'Farm fresh' on a market stall doesn't always mean the seller grew it. Some resell wholesale stock, so ask.
  • 'Natural' and 'organic-style' are marketing words. Only certified organic has a standard behind it.
  • Bulk only saves money if you use it. A 10 kg box of tomatoes that rots costs more than 1 kg from a stall.
  • Market hours are early, and wholesale halls close by 10:00 or 11:00. Don't arrive at noon.
  • Don't fill pots with garden soil. It compacts and drowns roots, so use potting mix.
  • UPF research shows strong links but rarely proves cause. Eat more whole food without getting scared of every packet.
